# Threading: complete mobile trajectory and original-model playback

The previous 216-candidate study included two grasp-face choices for this Threading source. Its stationary bases failed full-path IK. Moving the base alone still left the desired gripper intersecting the table. A constant 10-degree rotation about the calibrated pad contact line removes that hand/table obstruction while preserving both contact-point trajectories exactly.

The new solve uses the original source table, a bounded base XY/yaw and seven right-arm joints. It minimizes TCP error while constraining the original torso bars to remain at least 3 mm from the table. The solve starts at a geometrically feasible middle frame and follows the complete frozen retarget sequence backward and forward. It preserves all 1,257 reference times, object references and contact intents. The native recording has 203 samples at 20 Hz over 10.1 seconds. The existing retarget clock includes a 1.045883774 time-dilation factor and a two-second evaluation hold; this experiment adds no further timing change.

All rows pass fresh FK, joint, self and environment checks. Maximum position error is 1.06 micrometers; minimum joint margin is 31 mrad. A separate full-robot fixture check tests every row and the declared gripper-aperture envelope with 3 mm clearance. Non-distal hand geometry remains at least 7.657 mm from the reference needle. The original clock already satisfies the planning speed limits: maximum arm speed is 0.635 rad/s and base XY/yaw maxima are 0.221 m/s, 0.458 m/s and 0.791 rad/s. No retiming was applied.

Finite CAD pad-face projections overlap the unchanged 40 mm square handle by at least 99.61% and 99.09%, respectively, with at least 13.15 mm edge margin around the calibrated contact centers. The most distant pad corner extends 1.615 mm beyond the square. This is a supported projected area calculation, not a claim that the entire pad or a measured pressure patch is supported. Exact per-frame geometry evidence is stored in the three `nonbox-threading-mobile-roll10-*` JSON reports.

## Physical result

The fresh physical pilot reconstructs the original frozen scene, component grasp and pad-line rotation, verifies exact agreement with the solved reference arrays, and rechecks the complete fixture envelope. It executes the existing `contact005_velocity` controller, with a 0.05 rad closure offset and 0.03 rad servo margin, on unchanged source object geometry, mass, friction and contact parameters.

All 1,257 actual control rows execute. The independent actuator replay matches qpos, qvel and object poses exactly. The common state archive contains the complete issued joint-position controls, robot state, task-object poses and terminal state, without RGB.

The pilot **fails physical validation**: maximum grasp drift is 4.206 mm / 3.322 degrees and hand/object penetration reaches 1.860 mm. The task does not remain satisfied. All original environment, self, joint, velocity and complete-playback gates pass. The physical report is `nonbox-threading-mobile-roll10-physical-v1.json`.

The tripod is correctly initialized, settles about 5.17 mm vertically, and remains near its reset X coordinate through 8 seconds. During attempted insertion, actual needle contact moves and tips the tripod. Its ring ends about 59 mm away in X; the needle misses the actual ring by 67.9 mm. Carry tracking error also reaches 21.0 mm, predominantly from the moving base; replacing measured base coordinates with reference coordinates in offline FK reduces that error to 1.41 mm. These are measured failure causes, not grounds to move or fix the tripod artificially.

The geometry solve is pinned to release `af843d7673fec92230740ee90f28393a86120f2e694922ae4567afea61b1aad5`; physical playback and export use immutable release `ce7b7158da96c68ad857dfd9e3ebdeefa6cf64ca5fa2e008a98b71c33cac6fa7`. Original source provenance is the checksum-bound `source/threading.hdf5` at Hugging Face revision `33016f8a62c02334f929f2913af8fdd2a8a129e1`. All scripts, numeric solutions and attempted playback directories are retained in the local artifact backup. The first attempt's export rejected incomplete supplied provenance before writing measured arrays; its scene, plan, qualification and explicit failure record are retained. The second attempt supplies the complete original raw-file provenance and has the complete measured archive and replay.

## Isolated controller comparisons and acquisition cause

Four additional complete physical attempts retain the source model, reference and timing. Closure offsets 0.02 and 0.035 rad fail the same task, drift and hand/object penetration gates. Whole-source base velocity feedforward at 0.5 and 1.0 uses the original 0.05 rad closure and unchanged gains. Gain 1.0 reduces rotation drift to 2.714 degrees, but translation drift remains 3.888 mm and penetration 1.841 mm; the task still fails. All five measured attempts have complete uniform state archives and exact actuator replay. Archive inventories and checksums are in `nonbox-threading-mobile-artifacts-v1.json`.

The source records the tripod's settling trajectory. Immediately before first insertion contact, the source forecast and measured ring centers differ by only 89 micrometers. The desired needle centerline misses the measured ring center by 2.125 mm, whereas the actual needle misses by 9.818 mm. The observed 5.17 mm passive settling is already represented by the native source, so this evidence does not justify changing the fixture or its planned frame. See `nonbox-threading-ring-frame-v1.json`.

Acquisition is already offset before lift. With baseline base control, first bilateral contact places the calibrated pad midpoint 2.97 mm along the handle away from its intended center. At the source calibration anchor it remains 2.90 mm away. Base feedforward reduces these offsets to 0.95 and 0.91 mm, but the needle has rotated 2.52 degrees relative to its desired attachment by the anchor. A world-TCP guard alone cannot detect that object-relative error.

The new `box_acquisition` adapter qualifies the unchanged native handle patch and holds the robot target until measured world-TCP and object-relative alignment, then bilateral force, remain valid for 0.1 seconds. Each distal pad must measure at least 0.1 N for the unchanged 7.6 g needle. This guard does not replace any physical validation gate. Every original reference row is retained: original closure is delayed to row 278, entry and measured waits repeat that index, a 0.1-second exit blend consumes rows 279–288, and the remaining source resumes unchanged.

The first fixed-base weighted-IK proposal was rejected and retained. All 17 geometry-eligible pre-lift rows encountered the same wrist limit. Explicit independent pose/patch constraints recover the original earliest row without moving the base or reducing the 31 mrad planning joint margin. Minimax refinement reaches 0.715 mm / 0.007149 rad TCP error, 0.124 mm axial / 0.415 mm tangential patch error, 100% projected CAD-face support, and 19.55 mm contact-center edge margin. All 95 delayed-open, entry, closure-envelope and exit geometry samples pass, with non-distal object clearance at least 3 mm and peak ramp arm reference speed 0.576 rad/s. This is an admitted acquisition proposal, not a physical success; qualification and the prior rejection are both retained in `nonbox-threading-box-acquisition-*-v*.json`.

The original-model acquisition pilot uses release `fec986a6444eb00c79832b19239b6aa31328a694319351114b05cc6ad7edcfc3`, the baseline 0.05 rad closure, arm velocity feedforward 1 and base feedforward 0. It executes all 1,257 reference rows as 1,399 actual control intervals. Measured acquisition succeeds; independent replay recomputes every guard and source index, and complete state export passes. Physical validation still fails translation drift (3.863 mm), hand penetration (1.872 mm), and task stability. Rotation drift now passes at 2.586 degrees. See `nonbox-threading-box-acquisition-physical-v1.json`.

The remaining failure starts before insertion. Closing ends with 0.217 mm TCP error, 0.595 mm tangential patch error and 0.773 mm penetration. The exit blend ends at 0.932 mm penetration; the first 1 mm violation occurs at original row 304 while the handle still touches the table. Translation exceeds 3 mm at row 461 and worst penetration occurs at row 475; first needle/tripod contact follows at row 609. At the end of measured closure each pad supplies about 3.56 N normal force against the needle's 0.0746 N weight. Representative contact forces remain inside their unchanged friction cones. This supports examining overclosure and subsequent tracking, rather than moving the tripod or forcing insertion. Exact actuator-only contact evidence is in `nonbox-threading-box-acquisition-cause-v1.json`.

Two matched acquisition attempts reduce only the closure offset. At 0.02 rad, penetration falls to 1.331 mm but drift remains 4.536 mm / 3.586 degrees. At 0.005 rad, penetration passes the original threshold at 0.866 mm, while drift remains 4.029 mm / 3.584 degrees and the task still fails. Both retain complete source coverage, measured acquisition, exact replay and state export. All three attempts have exactly identical qpos, qvel, actuator commands and clocks before the first actual closure command at frame 382. Their inserted durations are respectively 1.42, 1.47 and 1.53 seconds; these differences are measured guard waits, not cropped source intervals. See `nonbox-threading-box-acquisition-comparison-v1.json` for exact events and comparison evidence.

An additional reference audit finds that, after calibration at source row 335, the intended object-relative TCP changes by up to 6.120 mm / 4.860 degrees; its calibrated pad midpoint moves 4.584 mm over the native handle. The maximum occurs at row 555, before measured insertion contact in the baseline acquisition attempt. This is a reference consistency issue and a possible source of task-pose mismatch. It does not by itself prove that the reference variation causes physical sliding. A separate geometry-only audit preserves the original object trajectory and tests a constant calibrated object-to-TCP attachment, with the original hand references archived and the existing measured acquisition/exit continuity retained. Drift exceeds 3 mm before ring contact, with further rotation after contact. The rigid-attachment proposal is retained as a failed counterfactual and is not selected as the default. Reports are `nonbox-threading-fixed-attachment-{geometry,physical,cause}-v1.json`.


## Post-acquisition tracking and measured contact geometry

Two isolated attempts use the original hand trajectory, 0.005 rad closure and the same measured acquisition, adding base velocity compensation only after every acquisition and exit row. Gains 0.5 and 1.0 reduce maximum carry TCP tracking error to 12.902 and 9.077 mm, but worsen drift to 5.759 mm / 6.808 degrees and 5.446 mm / 6.348 degrees. Neither satisfies the task. Both execute all 1,257 reference rows as 1,410 actual intervals. State, controls, clocks and source indices match the uncompensated attempt exactly until the first nonzero compensation at actual frame 443 / source row 290. Independent per-row compensation and clock recomputation errors are zero, as are actuator replay errors. The default remains zero; both failed comparisons are preserved in `nonbox-threading-post-acquisition-baseff-{audit,artifacts}-v1.json`.

Native contact witnesses were then audited on the original-hand, 0.005 rad closure attempt, with exact actuator replay error zero. First 3 mm drift occurs at source row 460; first needle/tripod contact follows at row 605. At the drift crossing, each pad carries about 0.63 N through three contact points concentrated near one end of its finite CAD face. The normal-force centroid is 1.40–1.43 mm from that boundary; the discrete contact-point span is 15.26 square millimeters compared with a 719.06 square millimeter projected hull. This span is not a measured pressure area. Boundary vertices alone are normal collision-manifold samples and do not establish a bad grasp; their concentration and changing load distribution are the relevant evidence.

The native pad planes have an intrinsic 1.997-degree toe-in. The unchanged linkage preserves that angle across apertures 0.8–1.1 rad, so changing closure cannot make both planes parallel to the box's opposed faces. At the original calibration anchor their normal bisector is already only 0.02695 degrees from the box-face normal. A common rigid rotation can balance approximately 0.999 degrees per pad but cannot flatten both planes. This rules out a meaningful cylinder-style facet snap as the next correction for this particular handle.

The native needle COM is at object Y = 47.368 mm. The measured bilateral normal-load centroid is Y = 47.807 mm near lift and 46.235 mm at the first drift crossing. Consequently, the load already acts within 0.44–1.13 mm of the COM, despite the nominal geometric patch center being at Y = 60 mm. Moving the nominal patch directly to the COM would not center the actual load. Near lift, contacts on opposite vertical pad edges generate a 19.164 mNm normal-force couple, but tangential forces reduce the total contact moment to 0.180 mNm. The larger normal-only number is not a net applied torque. Neither a blind COM shift nor a new force sweep is justified by these measurements.

The unchanged native elliptic constraints are converged at the audited interior-cone contacts: `J*qacc + R*force - reference_acceleration` is at floating-point precision, and the tangential reference acceleration equals `-damping * contact_velocity`. Damping is 171.821 per second. Nonzero tangential velocities therefore do not imply friction-cone saturation or a failed solve. At source row 460, pad-weighted tangent speeds are 1.03 and 2.42 mm/s, cone utilization is at most 0.081, and the regularizer-force terms are only 0.0073 and 0.0041 m/s² compared with constraint accelerations 0.170 and 0.420 m/s². This is a dynamic transient, not evidence that increasing friction or changing impedance would fix the unchanged-model task. The interpretation follows the [official MuJoCo constraint formulation](https://mujoco.readthedocs.io/en/stable/computation/index.html#parameters); no contact parameters were changed.

Full contact positions, native facet normals, finite hull margins, forces, tangent velocities, lever arms, solver residuals and scene hashes are retained in `nonbox-threading-native-contact-witness-v1.json`, `nonbox-threading-contact-lever-arms-v1.json`, `nonbox-threading-pad-opposition-geometry-v1.json` and `nonbox-threading-tangent-constraint-v1.json`. These diagnostics do not establish physical success or a new admitted grasp correction.
